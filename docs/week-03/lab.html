Week 03 Lab — One image, two destinations

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

What you are doing

Taking one supplied image to two different destinations, and writing down why each number is what it is.

Everyone works from the same file, so the image is not the variable — your reasoning is. That is deliberate: nobody fails this because their own photograph was unusable.

You are not making anything of your own today. No shooting, no campaign images. This week is procedure, on a file somebody else chose.

Part 1 · Name your destinations properly

  1. Take the two destination briefs you are given.
  2. Before you export anything, write each destination down concretely — a print size and stock, or a platform and placement.
  3. "Print" and "digital" are not destinations. They determine nothing, and if you write them you will not be able to justify a single number afterwards.
  4. Check both with a partner before moving on. If either of you cannot say what setting follows from a destination, it is not specific enough yet.

Part 2 · Export

  1. Work from one supplied file for both exports. Not two copies edited separately — one source, two deliveries.
  2. Produce one export per destination.
  3. For each, decide and record:
    • pixel dimensions and resolution
    • file format
    • colour system
  4. For each of those three, write why — for that destination. Not what the setting is; why that destination needs it.

Part 3 · Check what you actually delivered

This is what the assignment is marked on.

  1. Reopen both exported files.
  2. Read the numbers off the files themselves — not off the export dialogue, not from memory.
  3. Compare each against what you wrote. Where they disagree, fix the file or fix the statement until they match.
  4. Swap statements with a partner. Their job is to find one number that is not justified by the destination. There is almost always one.

Before you leave

  • Both delivered files have been reopened and checked against your statements. This is the assessment, and it is the one part of the lab you cannot do afterwards from memory.
  • Your two destinations are written down concretely. You will need them when you write the assignment up.
