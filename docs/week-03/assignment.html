Week 03 Assignment — Two destinations, one file

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

Module summative · 10% of your course grade.

This is the largest single weekly grade in the course, and it is the rehearsal for campaign component D — the output package, worth 15% of your campaign. The procedure you use here is the procedure you will use there, on your own image, in week 7.

The direction study — Phase 2 — is also due this week. See What to submit.

What to submit

1 Both exports, one per destination, from the one supplied file
2 A specification statement for each — pixel dimensions and resolution, file format, colour system, and the reason for each
3 Both destinations named concretely — a print size and stock, a platform and placement
4 Phase 2 — the direction study: three visual directions, every generated output retained, one line per rejection, and your intent statement

Items 1–3 are the 10%. Item 4 is scored on the project rubric as component B.

What a complete answer looks like

Destination A — A3 poster, uncoated 170gsm, pinned in a corridor.

Chosen Why, for this destination
Pixels / resolution 3510 × 4950 px at 300 ppi A3 is 11.7 × 16.5 in; at 300 ppi that is the pixel count it needs. Viewed close, so detail matters
Format TIFF Lossless, and the file is going to a printer rather than over a network. Size is not a constraint here
Colour system CMYK It is being printed in ink. Converting now lets me see the gamut loss before it is a surprise

Destination B — Instagram feed post.

Chosen Why, for this destination
Pixels / resolution 1080 × 1350 px The platform's feed size. Anything larger is discarded on upload, and I would rather choose the downscale than let the platform choose it
Format JPG, quality 85 It will be re-compressed on upload regardless. A large lossless file gains nothing and costs upload time
Colour system sRGB Screens, and the narrowest safe screen profile — it looks the same on a bad phone as on a good one

What that demonstrates: every one of the six settings differs between the two destinations, and each difference is traced to a requirement of that destination rather than to a habit. Nothing here is a rule recited — the reasoning could be redone for a third destination without asking anybody.

What does not count

  • "Print" and "digital" as destinations. They determine no setting. If your destination cannot decide a number, you have not named one.
  • Identical specifications for two different destinations. Two technically correct but identical exports demonstrate a default, not an understanding — and detecting that is what the two-destination structure exists for.
  • A difference in resolution alone, with format and colour system left at one setting for both.
  • A statement that does not match the delivered file. This gets checked by opening the files. Write the numbers from the file, not from the export dialogue.
  • Reasons that describe the setting rather than justifying it. "CMYK because it is a print file" restates the choice. "CMYK because it is being printed in ink and I want to see the gamut loss now rather than on the proof" justifies it.

Use the glossary

The terms that matter this week: destination, specification statement, pixel dimensions, resolution, ppi, dpi, file format, colour system, gamut, export. All are in the Glossary under Module 2.

Rubric

LO 2 — Resolution, format, colour

Demonstrate an understanding of image resolution, file formats, and color systems in the production of digital graphics.

Level
Exceeds The destinations are named precisely enough to determine the settings — a stated print size and stock, a stated platform and placement — and the reasoning follows from those rather than from convention
Meets The two specifications differ, each difference is justified by what that destination requires, and the delivered files match what was written
Approaching Correct-looking but identical specifications for two different destinations, or a difference in resolution alone while format and colour system sit at one default
Not yet The delivered file contradicts the stated specification, or a destination is named as "print" or "digital" and determines nothing

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors: competency rubric.
