Week 02 Assignment — The campaign brief

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

Module summative · 5% of your course grade.

The 5% is the judgment discussion in the lab, which was observed and marked in the room. This document is what you submit alongside it: Phase 1 of your campaign, plus the record of your generation work.

Do not read "week 2" as a gentle start. Phase 1 is the foundation the other five phases sit on, and a brief written about the organization instead of the audience produces four weeks of images aimed at nobody.

What to submit

1 The campaign brief — organization, awareness gap, audience, where they encounter the campaign, ethical commitments
2 The intent statement you wrote before generating
3 Every generated output, numbered in the order produced — kept and rejected
4 One line per rejected output, saying why it was wrong for that intent
5 A disclosure statement — which images are yours, where anything else came from, and how generative AI was used

Items 2–4 are not graded this week. They are collected now because they become the direction study, due next week and worth 15% of your campaign, and because they cannot be reconstructed later.

What a complete answer looks like

An awareness gap, written as a gap:

People in this town assume the food pantry serves people who are not working. Two-thirds of the households it serves have at least one person in full-time employment. The photographs the pantry currently uses show empty shelves and volunteers — nothing that shows who actually comes through the door, and nothing that would change what somebody believes.

An audience, written as a person:

A working parent in their thirties who drives past the building twice a day and has never been inside. They encounter the campaign on a poster at the leisure centre and in their local Facebook group. They are not unsympathetic. They think it is for somebody else.

An ethical commitment, written as a decision they will actually face:

I will not photograph anyone's face without asking them first and telling them where the image will appear. If somebody agrees and then looks uncomfortable while I am shooting, I will stop. I will not make the building look busier or emptier than it was on the day.

What that demonstrates: the gap is specific enough that any proposed image can be tested against it, the audience is a person in a situation rather than a demographic, and the commitments anticipate a decision that will actually come up in week 4 rather than restating a general principle.

What does not count

  • A gap written as a goal. "Raise awareness", "increase visibility", "tell their story" — none of these can be used to judge an image. If you cannot ask does this photograph close the gap, it is not a gap.
  • A brief about the organization. Their history, their mission statement, their services. The organization is not who needs convincing.
  • An audience that is a demographic. "The community", "young people", "local residents". Name a person, where they are, and what they currently believe.
  • Ethical commitments that are general principles. "I will be respectful" commits you to nothing. Name a decision and say what you will do when it arrives.
  • Only kept generations submitted. Operation of a tool is demonstrated; intent is not. This one cannot be fixed after the fact.
  • Rejection reasons that are interchangeable. "Didn't like it", "not right", "wrong feel" — six of those tell a reader nothing about what you were after.

Use the glossary

The terms that matter this week: provenance, misrepresentation, generative AI, input, retention, disclosure statement. All are in the Glossary under Module 1.

Rubric

The 5% is marked on the observed judgment discussion. The brief itself is Phase 1 of your campaign and is scored on the project rubric as component A.

LO 5 — Evaluating images

Evaluate digital images for clarity, ethical use, and appropriateness in professional design contexts.

Level
Exceeds Raises an ethical problem in an image with no visible defect and can say what makes it a problem rather than a preference
Meets Addresses clarity, ethical use, and appropriateness, distinguishing stated preference from defensible judgment
Approaching Fluent on clarity, silent on provenance and fit until prompted — a third of the outcome, delivered confidently
Not yet Reaction in place of evaluation
