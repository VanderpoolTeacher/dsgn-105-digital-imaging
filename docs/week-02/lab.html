This week is the exception. Usually the lab is where being wrong is free. Part 2 of this lab is observed and marked, because what it assesses exists only while it is happening and leaves nothing behind to submit.

What you are doing

Two things, in this order.

First you generate images against an intention you write down beforehand, and keep every single output — including the ones you would rather nobody saw. Those rejects are what you will hand in next week as part of the direction study.

Then you look at a set of images you did not make, and say what, if anything, is wrong with them.

You are not making any campaign images today. No photographs, no editing. This week is entirely about where images come from and what may be done to them — the making starts in week 4.

Part 1 · Generate to a stated intent

  1. Write your intent first. One sentence naming what the image has to do — not what it should look like. Hand it in before you generate anything; an intent written afterwards is a description.
  2. Generate against that intent. Work in whatever direction you like.
  3. Save every output. Number them in the order they came out. Nothing gets deleted, including the obviously bad ones.
  4. For each output you reject, write one line: why it was wrong for this intent. Write it at the time, not at the end. Reasons reconstructed later are invented later.
  5. Keep going until you have a result you would defend, or until you have decided the intent itself was wrong — which is a legitimate finding and worth writing down.

Your rejects are graded evidence next week. They are the only place your intention is visible, and they cannot be recreated once deleted.

Part 2 · Judge the supplied set

This part is observed and marked.

  1. You will be given a set of images you did not make, with no information about them.
  2. Work through them and say what, if anything, is wrong with each. Some of them are fine.
  3. Cover three things every time:
    • Clarity — does the image communicate what it appears to be for?
    • Ethical use — provenance, permission, whether a viewer would conclude something untrue
    • Appropriateness — does it fit the professional context it is going into?
  4. Say it out loud. This is a discussion, not a worksheet.
  5. Be specific enough that somebody could act on it. "This crop removes the context that made the subject's situation legible" is a judgment. "AI raises questions about authenticity" is a position, and it says nothing about the image on screen.

You will not be told what to look for. That is deliberate and it is what is being assessed.

Before you leave

  • Your retained-output folder has been checked. A folder holding only your kept images is the one failure in this lab that cannot be fixed afterwards — and next week's direction study is 15% of your campaign.
  • Your intent statement is handed in, the one you wrote before generating.
  • Your campaign brief is on track. It is due before next week's session and it is Phase 1 of your campaign.
