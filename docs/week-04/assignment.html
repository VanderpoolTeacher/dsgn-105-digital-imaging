Week 04 Assignment — The reading-order test

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

Module summative · 5% of your course grade.

This is the rehearsal for campaign component E — the reading-order test on your own lead image, worth 15% of your campaign, in week 7. Same procedure, higher stakes, and by then it will not be new.

What to submit

1 The image you tested, from the supplied set
2 Your intended reading order — first, second, third — with the mechanism named for each
3 The exact questions you asked, word for word
4 What each viewer said, verbatim — at least two viewers, from outside this class
5 Your revision: what you would change, and which mechanism you would change it with

Item 3 is what the grade is on. Items 2, 4 and 5 are required and are not where the 5% sits.

What a complete answer looks like

Intended order

Element Mechanism
First The figure at the left edge Isolation — everything else in the frame is clustered
Second The doorway behind them Direction — their gaze runs across to it
Third The lettering on the glass Contrast — dark type on the one bright surface

Questions asked, exactly:

  1. "What do you notice first?"
  2. "And after that?"
  3. "Anything else?"

What viewers said:

Viewer 1: "The bright window. Then the person. Then… the writing, I think." Viewer 2: "The person on the left. Then the window. I didn't read the writing at all."

Revision:

Both viewers found the window before or instead of the doorway, and neither of them got to the lettering the way I expected. The window is the brightest thing in the frame by a distance, so contrast is overriding the direction I was relying on. I would darken the window, which should let the gaze do its work — and it also explains why the lettering was missed, because it was competing with the same bright surface.

What that demonstrates: the questions name nothing and could not have produced these answers by suggestion; the mechanisms are named rather than the elements; the contradicting result is recorded rather than argued with; and the revision identifies which mechanism was actually operating instead of simply making the intended element bigger.

What does not count

  • Questions that name something. "Do you see the sign?" and "This is about the volunteer — where does your eye go?" both put the answer in the viewer's head. The result then looks like a successful test and measures nothing.
  • Testing on classmates. They know the vocabulary and may know the image.
  • One viewer. Two viewers who disagree is a real finding. One viewer cannot disagree with anybody.
  • Attributing the order to the subject. "They looked at the only face first" is a true observation and not evidence about your composition. Ask whether you could have changed it.
  • Recording only the answers you wanted. The contradicting ones carry all the information.
  • A revision that just makes the intended thing bigger. Scale is one of five mechanisms and it is rarely the one that was failing.

Use the glossary

The terms that matter this week: composition, visual hierarchy, reading order, reading-order test, leading, compositional mechanism, isolation. All are in the Glossary under Module 3.

Rubric

LO 3 — Composition and hierarchy

Apply compositional principles and visual hierarchy in the creation of digital imagery.

This week evidences the testing half. Applying it to an image you composed yourself is campaign component E, in week 7.

Level
Exceeds Acts on a result that contradicted the intent, and can say what the test revealed about their own habits rather than about this image
Meets States an intended reading order before testing, runs the test without leading the viewer, and names the compositional mechanism producing the order
Approaching The order is attributed to the subject rather than the arrangement, or the viewer was told what to look for before being asked what they saw
Not yet No independent viewer. The student's own certainty stands in for the test

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors: competency rubric.
