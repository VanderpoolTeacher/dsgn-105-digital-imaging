Week 04 Lab — Test an image you did not make, then start shooting

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

What you are doing

Three things.

You run a reading-order test on an image you did not make — which keeps it about the method rather than about defending your own work. You start shooting your campaign, which you will be doing for the next three weeks. And you take part in the first critique session of the course, on the direction studies submitted last week.

Part 1 · Before you show anyone anything

  1. Take one image from the supplied found-image set.
  2. Write down the reading order you believe it produces — first, second, third.
  3. For each, name the mechanism responsible: scale, contrast, placement, direction, or isolation.
  4. Write out the exact questions you will ask, word for word.
  5. Hand in steps 2–4 now, before you test anything. (These are what you are graded on.)

Part 2 · Run the test

  1. Find at least two viewers from outside this class. Not classmates — they know the vocabulary and may know the image.
  2. Ask your written questions, in order. Do not add anything.
  3. Write down what they said, verbatim, including answers that contradict what you expected.
  4. If they ask what the image is about, say you will tell them afterwards — then do.

Part 3 · Check each other's questions

  1. In pairs, read each other's question sets aloud.
  2. Mark any question that told the viewer what to look for.
  3. Almost every first draft has one. Rewrite and retest before you submit.

Part 4 · Start shooting

  1. Write a one-sentence intent for what you are photographing before you go.
  2. Shoot. More than you think you need.
  3. Back up your originals untouched the moment you are back at a machine. You submit them in week 6 and there is no way to recover them.

Part 5 · Critique session 1

On the direction studies submitted last week. For each piece of work, cover three things:

  • Clarity — does it communicate what it appears to be for?
  • Ethical use — provenance, permission, whether a viewer would conclude something untrue
  • Appropriateness — does it fit where it is going?

Be specific enough that the person could act on it tomorrow.

Before you leave

  • Your intended order, your mechanisms, and your written questions are handed in — before you tested anything.
  • A partner has read your questions aloud and you have fixed any that led.
  • You have taken photographs. Not planned to; taken.
