The Awareness Campaign — Rubric

Step 3b of the Course Build Process

Covers every component except the critique record, which is scored on its own rubric.

A. Campaign brief — 10%

Evidences: Container — evidences no outcome

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The gap is stated so precisely that any proposed image can be tested against it. The audience is a specific person in a specific situation. Ethical commitments anticipate a decision the student will actually face while shooting.
Meets Names the organization, a specific awareness gap, an identified audience with the contexts they encounter the campaign in, and ethical commitments governing how subjects are represented.
Approaching The gap is written as a goal rather than a gap — "raise awareness", "increase visibility" — so no image can be judged against it. Audience is a demographic rather than a person.
Not yet Describes the organization and its mission without identifying what the audience wrongly believes, or omits ethical commitments entirely.

B. Direction study — 15%

Evidences: CLO 6

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The rejection reasons trace a visible line of thinking — later rejects fail for different reasons than earlier ones. The ethical judgment identifies something specific the tool did to this subject that a general policy would not have caught.
Meets Three visually distinct directions. All generated outputs retained, kept and rejected, each rejection reasoned. A statement of the intent the generation served and the ethical judgment applied to what it produced, specific to the material generated.
Approaching Rejects are present but the reasons are interchangeable — "didn't like it", "not right" — so the intent behind the selection stays invisible. Or the three directions are one idea in three colour treatments.
Not yet Only kept outputs submitted, so operation of the tool is demonstrated but intent is not. Or the ethics statement is a general position on AI that says nothing about these images.

C. Campaign image set — 25%

Evidences: CLO 1, CLO 4

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The consistent treatment is doing work — it carries meaning across the set rather than only matching. Layer structure is legible enough that another person could pick the file up and continue it.
Meets Five to seven photographs, submitted with unedited originals and layered working files. The layer structure accounts for the visible result and edits are held non-destructively. Before-and-after pairs show the edit changed what the photograph communicates, not only its cleanliness. The treatment holds across the set so the images read as one campaign.
Approaching Each image is well edited on its own but the treatment drifts across the set — the images are individually strong and collectively unrelated. Or the edit is technically clean and communicates nothing the original did not.
Not yet Originals not submitted, so improvement cannot be judged. Or files are flattened, leaving no record of how the image was built.

D. Output package — 15%

Evidences: CLO 2

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The two destinations are named at a level of specificity that determines the settings — a stated print size and stock, a stated platform and placement — and the reasoning follows from those rather than from convention.
Meets One image taken from a single working file to a print destination and a screen destination. Each export carries a statement of pixel dimensions and resolution, file format, and colour system, with the reason for each. The specifications differ between destinations, each difference justified by that destination's requirements, and the delivered files match the stated specifications.
Approaching Both destinations receive correct-looking but identical specifications, or specifications that differ by resolution alone while format and colour system are left at one default.
Not yet The delivered files contradict the stated specifications, or one destination is missing.

E. Reading-order test and revision — 15%

Evidences: CLO 3

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The revision is applied across the set rather than to the tested image alone, and the student can say what the test revealed about their compositional habits.
Meets A stated intended reading order, the orders viewers actually reported without being told the intent, and the resulting revision. The student names the compositional choice — scale, contrast, placement, direction, isolation — that produces the order, rather than the subject matter.
Approaching Viewers were shown the image after being told what to look at, so the reported order confirms the intent and measures nothing. Or the order is attributed to the subject — the only face, the only text — which the student did not arrange.
Not yet No independent viewer. The student's own account of the reading order, or the instructor's agreement that the image is well composed, stands in for the test.

G. Campaign walkthrough — 5%

Evidences: Container — evidences no outcome

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The walkthrough is structured by the campaign's argument rather than by the order the images were made.
Meets Every image is covered — what was intended, what was done, how it serves the campaign — and the student answers questions about images they did not plan to dwell on.
Approaching Covers the images the student is pleased with and passes quickly over the rest.
Not yet Incomplete, or describes what was done without connecting any of it to the campaign.
