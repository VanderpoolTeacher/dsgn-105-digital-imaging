DSGN 105 — Competency Rubric

The master rubric for every assignment in the course.

Six competencies, one per course learning outcome, four levels each. Every weekly assignment cites from here rather than inventing its own criteria.

Outcomes are reproduced verbatim from the course specification. Terms follow the Glossary.

How to use it

An assignment does not assess all six. Most assess one. Week 2 assesses two.

Each assignment states which competencies it evidences and what the week's specific evidence is. The level descriptors below do not change week to week — that is the point of a competency rubric. What changes is the artifact the student produces.

Level Means
Exceeds The competency is demonstrated, and the student can say what the comparison revealed
Meets The competency is demonstrated, with the comparison it is judged against
Approaching Half of it, or the artifact without the comparison
Not yet Not demonstrated

Same four levels as the project rubric and the critique rubric, so a student meets one scheme across the whole course.

The line between Meets and Exceeds

Almost always the same line: Meets supplies the second term. Exceeds says what the second term revealed.

Every outcome in this course needs a second term, and this is the single most distinctive fact about assessing it. A finished image is never sufficient evidence on its own:

Outcome The second term
LO 1 The layered working file behind the export
LO 2 The delivered file, checked against the stated specification
LO 3 A viewer who was not told the intent
LO 4 The unedited original
LO 5 Work the student did not make
LO 6 The generations that were rejected

A student who submits only the polished result has not produced weak evidence — they have produced none. Approaching is where the artifact arrives without its comparison, and it is the most common failure in this course.

A note on wording

These descriptors are written in plainer language than the outcomes they assess. They never say anything different. Where a descriptor and its outcome appear to diverge, the outcome is correct and the descriptor is a defect — report it rather than teaching to the rubric.

LO 1 — Raster software

Use industry-standard software to create, edit, and manipulate raster-based digital images for print and screen.

The competency: building an image so that how it was built is still visible and still changeable.

Level
Exceeds The layer structure is legible enough that another person could pick the file up and continue it, and the student can say why it is organised that way
Meets The layer structure accounts for the visible result, edits are held non-destructively, and both a print and a screen export come from the one working file
Approaching The image is well made but the file is thin — a stack that does not explain the result, or one export where the outcome names two
Not yet A flattened file. Nothing distinguishes it from an image that was downloaded

LO 2 — Resolution, format, colour

Demonstrate an understanding of image resolution, file formats, and color systems in the production of digital graphics.

The competency: reasoning from a destination to a number, rather than recalling the number.

Level
Exceeds The destinations are named precisely enough to determine the settings — a stated print size and stock, a stated platform and placement — and the reasoning follows from those rather than from convention
Meets The two specifications differ, each difference is justified by what that destination requires, and the delivered files match what was written
Approaching Correct-looking but identical specifications for two different destinations, or a difference in resolution alone while format and colour system sit at one default
Not yet The delivered file contradicts the stated specification, or a destination is named as "print" or "digital" and determines nothing

LO 3 — Composition and hierarchy

Apply compositional principles and visual hierarchy in the creation of digital imagery.

The competency: arranging an image so a stranger reads it in the intended order — and finding out whether they did.

Level
Exceeds Acts on a result that contradicted the intent, applies the change beyond the tested image, and can say what the test revealed about their own habits
Meets States an intended reading order before testing, runs the test without leading the viewer, and names the compositional mechanism producing the order
Approaching The order is attributed to the subject rather than to the arrangement — the only face, the only text — or the viewer was told what to look for before being asked what they saw
Not yet No independent viewer. The student's own certainty, or the instructor's agreement, stands in for the test

LO 4 — Editing and retouching

Edit and retouch photographs to enhance visual clarity, consistency, and communicative impact.

The competency: changing what a photograph says, and holding one treatment across a set.

Level
Exceeds The treatment carries meaning across the set rather than only matching, and the student can say what each image now says that its original did not
Meets Before-and-after pairs show the edit changed what the photograph communicates, not only its cleanliness, and the treatment holds across the whole set
Approaching Each image is well retouched on its own but the treatment drifts across the set, or the edit is technically cleaner and communicates nothing new
Not yet The unedited originals are absent, so improvement cannot be judged at all

LO 5 — Evaluating images

Evaluate digital images for clarity, ethical use, and appropriateness in professional design contexts.

The competency: judging work you did not make, across all three dimensions, specifically enough to act on.

Level
Exceeds Raises an ethical problem in an image with no visible defect and can say what makes it a problem rather than a preference
Meets Addresses clarity, ethical use, and appropriateness, distinguishing stated preference from defensible judgment
Approaching Fluent on clarity, silent on provenance and fit until prompted — a third of the outcome, delivered confidently
Not yet Reaction in place of evaluation, or evaluation only of the student's own work

LO 6 — Generative AI

Use generative AI tools to create, remix, and enhance digital images, applying ethical considerations and creative intent.

The competency: directing a tool toward an intention, and being able to show the intention existed.

Level
Exceeds The rejection reasons trace a visible line of thinking, and the ethical judgment identifies something specific the tool did to this subject that a general policy would not have caught
Meets Every output retained, kept and rejected, each rejection reasoned, with a statement of the intent the generation served and the ethical judgment applied to it
Approaching Rejects are present but the reasons are interchangeable, so the intent behind the selection stays invisible — or the ethics statement is a general position on AI that says nothing about these images
Not yet Only kept outputs submitted. Operation of a tool is demonstrated; intent is not

What this rubric does not cover

Photography. No outcome in this course names capture, so no competency here assesses it. The campaign requires the student's own photographs and none of them are graded as photographs — this is recorded in the summative design and is deliberate, not an omission.

The campaign's container components. The campaign brief and the walkthrough evidence no outcome and are scored on the project rubric only.
