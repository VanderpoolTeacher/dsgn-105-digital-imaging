Awareness Campaign — Critique Rubric

Step 3c of the Course Build Process

Scored independently of the student's own campaign. 15% of the project.

Evidences: CLO 5

Why this is separate

Making images and judging images are different competencies. Averaging them lets a strong maker pass an outcome about evaluation they have not met, and penalises a sharp critic whose own photographs are still developing. A student may score high here and low on the project rubric, or the reverse — both are meaningful results, not errors.

It scores critique of other students' work, never the student's own. Self-assessment confounds critical skill with self-knowledge; a student may be strong at one and weak at the other in either direction, and the two cannot be separated from the inside.

This is also the only component in the project with no artifact of its own. It exists in the room, which is why it recurs and why every session leaves a record.

Coverage of all three dimensions

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Treats the three dimensions as connected — shows how an ethical problem or a mismatch with context is causing the clarity problem, rather than listing three separate observations.
Meets Addresses clarity, ethical use, and appropriateness to the professional context in each critique.
Approaching Addresses clarity thoroughly and touches the other two only when prompted. Clarity sits on the surface of an image; the other two require knowing where it came from and where it is going.
Not yet Discusses only what is visible in the frame. Fluent about composition and exposure, silent on provenance and fit.

Depth of ethical judgment

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Raises an ethical problem in an image with no visible defect — a manipulation that misleads, an undisclosed source, a subject used rather than served — and can say what makes it a problem rather than a preference.
Meets Identifies ethical questions in the work under discussion and states what is at stake for the subject, the audience, or the organization.
Approaching Recognises ethical issues once they have been pointed out, but does not surface them independently. Spotting a flaw that announces itself shows observation; spotting one that does not shows evaluation.
Not yet Ethics enters only as a general remark about honesty in imagery, unattached to the image on screen.

Specificity and defensibility

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The critique changes what its recipient does next, and the recipient can act on it without asking a follow-up question.
Meets Observations are specific enough to act on, and the student distinguishes stated preference from defensible judgment — saying which is which.
Approaching Specific about what is wrong, vague about why it matters, so the recipient knows something is off but not what would fix it. Or judgment and taste are presented as the same kind of claim.
Not yet Reaction in place of evaluation — what the student likes, without a standard behind it.

Questions

Questioning is part of the assessment, not a formality afterwards. It is what separates a judgment the student holds from a phrase they have learned to produce.

The instructor asks follow-ups during the session — typically why a stated problem matters, what the alternative would be, or whether a judgment would still hold for a different audience. Expect to be asked about an image you did not choose to comment on.

Records are made at the time. A critique that is not logged as it happens leaves this outcome with no evidence at all — there is no file to go back to.
