Week 07 Assignment — Phases 4 and 5

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

Formative · 0% of your course grade — and these are Phases 4 and 5, worth 30% of your campaign between them.

Both are scored on the project rubric, as components D and E. Each is the sole evidence for one of your course outcomes — component D for resolution, formats and colour; component E for composition and hierarchy. There is nothing else behind either of them.

What to submit

Phase 4 — the output package

1 Both exports, from one working file
2 A specification statement for each — pixel dimensions and resolution, file format, colour system, and why for that destination
3 Both destinations named concretely
4 Confirmation the print destination was actually printed

Phase 5 — the reading-order test

5 Your intended reading order for the lead image, with the mechanism named for each
6 The exact questions you asked
7 What each viewer said, verbatim — at least two, from outside this class
8 Your revision, and where else in the set the same habit appears

What a complete answer looks like

Phase 5, the part students most often get wrong:

Intended: first the figure at the left (isolation), then the doorway (direction, their gaze), then the lettering (contrast).

Questions asked: "What do you notice first?" / "And after that?" / "Anything else?"

Viewer 1: "The white wall on the right. Then the person. I don't think I noticed any writing." Viewer 2: "The person, then the bright bit on the right, then the door."

Revision: Both viewers went to the bright wall, one of them before the figure. Contrast is beating the isolation I was relying on, and it also explains why the lettering was missed — it's competing with the same bright surface. I'm bringing the wall down about a stop and a half.

Looking across the set, three of my other six images have the same problem: a bright background area I didn't notice because I was looking at the subject. Two of them I'll bring down the same way. The third one the brightness is doing something I want, so I'm leaving it and I know why.

What that demonstrates: the questions name nothing; the contradicting result is recorded rather than argued with; the revision identifies the mechanism actually operating rather than making the intended element bigger; and the student has looked for the same habit across the set — which is what separates a finding about a photograph from a finding about how they compose.

What does not count

  • A print specification for a print that was never printed. The one part of component D that cannot be checked on screen.
  • Settings chosen because the image looks better. That is a preference. A specification is a reason belonging to the destination.
  • A statement that does not match the delivered file. These get opened.
  • Testing on classmates, or explaining the campaign before asking.
  • Running the test again until somebody agrees with you. If you keep going until you get the answer you wanted, you have measured nothing.
  • Arguing with a contradicting result. The viewer is not wrong; the image reads how it reads.
  • A revision applied only to the tested image. The test is about how you compose. Next week's outcome is applying it across the set, and it starts here.

Use the glossary

From Module 2: destination, specification statement, pixel dimensions, resolution, file format, colour system, export. From Module 3: reading order, reading-order test, leading, compositional mechanism. From Module 4: sequence. All in the Glossary.

Rubric

LO 2 — Resolution, format, colour (component D)

Demonstrate an understanding of image resolution, file formats, and color systems in the production of digital graphics.

Level
Exceeds The destinations are named precisely enough to determine the settings, and the reasoning follows from those rather than from convention
Meets The two specifications differ, each difference is justified by what that destination requires, and the delivered files match what was written
Approaching Correct-looking but identical specifications, or a difference in resolution alone
Not yet The delivered file contradicts the statement, or a destination determines nothing

LO 3 — Composition and hierarchy (component E)

Apply compositional principles and visual hierarchy in the creation of digital imagery.

Level
Exceeds Acts on a contradicting result, applies the change beyond the tested image, and can say what the test revealed about their own habits
Meets States an intended order before testing, runs the test without leading, and names the mechanism producing the order
Approaching The order is attributed to the subject, or the viewer was told what to look for
Not yet No independent viewer

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors: competency rubric.
