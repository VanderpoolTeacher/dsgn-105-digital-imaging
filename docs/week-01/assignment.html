Week 01 Assignment — The layered composition

DSGN 105 · Digital Imaging

Formative · submitted and critiqued · 0% of your course grade.

It is required. Zero percent does not mean optional. Under the formative requirement, this must be submitted before week 2's module summative, and it is accepted late without penalty up until that point. After it, it is not accepted at all.

There is a reason it is stated this plainly. Nothing later in the course physically needs this file — you could skip this week and still sit every assessment that follows. What you would be skipping is the only low-stakes attempt at a habit that is graded three more times: in week 5's composite, and in the campaign image set, which is 25% of your campaign.

What to submit

1 The working file — layered, groups intact, adjustment layers live
2 One export of the composition, any common format
3 Two or three sentences naming what your partner could not read in your file, and what you changed

Submit to your course folder before week 2's session.

What a complete answer looks like

A composition of six supplied elements in three groups — background, subject, type — with a Curves adjustment layer above the subject group, revised once after it was first applied.

The note reads:

My partner could not tell what was in the top group because I had called it stuff. They also thought the adjustment was merged into the subject because I had put it inside the group rather than above it. I renamed the group type and moved the adjustment out so it sits above everything it affects.

What that demonstrates: the file is organised around what things are rather than what was done to them, the adjustment is genuinely reversible and positioned where a reader would look for it, and the student found both of those out from a reader rather than from their own judgment — which is the only way either can be found out.

What does not count

  • A flattened file. There is nothing to assess. A merged composition cannot be distinguished from an image you downloaded, and the outcome is about how it was built.
  • A beautiful composition with an unreadable stack. Nine layers named Layer 14 copy 3 fail this assignment however good the picture is.
  • An adjustment layer you never changed. The outcome is reversibility. A setting applied once and left alone shows you can add an adjustment, not that you can revise one.
  • Groups made at the end. Usually visible, and it misses the point — the structure is meant to be how you think while building, not a tidy-up afterwards.
  • A note that says your partner understood everything. Possible, and rare. If it happens, say what they asked about first and why that part was the least obvious.

Use the glossary

The terms that matter this week: raster, layer, adjustment layer, non-destructive, working file, flattened. All are in the Glossary under Module 1.

Use them in your note. "I made the top bit clearer" and "I moved the adjustment layer above the group it affects" describe the same action, and only one of them is evidence.

Rubric

LO 1 — Raster software

Use industry-standard software to create, edit, and manipulate raster-based digital images for print and screen.

This week evidences the construction half only. Print and screen destinations arrive in week 3 and are assessed in the campaign's output package.

Level
Exceeds The stack is legible enough that a partner could continue it without asking anything, and the student can say why it is organised that way
Meets The layer structure accounts for the visible result, groups are named for what things are, and the adjustment layer is live and was revised
Approaching The image is well made but the file is thin — an ungrouped stack, or an adjustment applied once and never revisited
Not yet A flattened file. Nothing distinguishes it from an image that was downloaded

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors: competency rubric.
