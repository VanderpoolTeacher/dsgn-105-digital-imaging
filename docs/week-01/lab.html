What you are doing

You are building one composition out of supplied parts, and then handing your machine to somebody else to see whether they can carry on with it.

The composition itself does not matter much. The subject is unimportant and nobody is grading how it looks. What is being built is a file — one whose structure explains the image to a person who was not there when you made it.

You are also choosing the nonprofit your campaign will be built for. That decision is small today and expensive to delay: the campaign brief is due at the end of next week.

Part 1 · Build the composition

  1. Open the supplied element pack. It holds 6–8 images at mixed sizes, cleared for use in class.
  2. Combine at least five of them into one composition, on a canvas of any size you like.
  3. Make layer groups as you go, not at the end. Grouping a finished mess teaches you nothing and takes longer.
  4. Name every group after what it is in the image — background, subject, type — not after what you did to make it.
  5. Add at least one adjustment layer, and then change it at least once after you have applied it. Reversibility is what you are demonstrating; a setting you never revisited demonstrates nothing.

Part 2 · Swap machines

This is the part of the lab that matters most.

  1. Swap machines with a partner. Open their file, not yours.
  2. Without asking them anything, work out and say out loud:
    • what the top group contains
    • how to reverse their adjustment
  3. If you cannot answer both from the file alone, tell them. They revise now, in the room, while it is still cheap.
  4. Swap back and fix whatever your partner could not read.

Do not explain your file to your partner before they open it. The explanation is exactly what will not be available when this is assessed.

Part 3 · Choose your organization

  1. Take an organization from the nonprofit shortlist, or propose one of your own for approval.
  2. Note which written brief you are working from. You get one for every organization on the list, so you are never waiting on somebody to reply to an email before you can start.
  3. Write down one question you want the organization's own material to answer.

Before you leave

  • Your partner has read your file and you have fixed what they could not. This cannot be done later — it needs two people and two machines.
  • Your organization is chosen and recorded. If you are proposing your own, submit it for approval today; approval takes time this course does not have much of.
